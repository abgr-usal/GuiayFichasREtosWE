## Methodological Orientation

The WE CHALLENGES aim to develop STEAM skills and cover various topics related to the daily lives of students at different educational levels, seeing the usefulness of the content worked on contextually in real-life situations. These challenges can be addressed in the classroom using an active methodology in which students are the protagonists of their own learning.

To face these challenges, they can be presented in a motivating way by placing the students in the role of scientists (engineer, architect...). These challenges can be carried out in small or large groups. Collaborative work is always more enriching, so we suggest forming groups of 3-4 students. These groups should be heterogeneous, made up of boys and girls with different characteristics and abilities so that everyone can contribute their bit to the cooperative work.

Faced with any of these challenges, and depending on the educational level, one can start by encouraging students to make explicit: what are they asking me in the challenge? what do I know about the topic? or what do I need to know? The problem can also be broken down into smaller parts and analyzed progressively.

In the process of solving the challenge, the work of each group can be reviewed and the activity guided with questions that guide the students, especially when designing and validating the required model. The questions can be along the lines of: what do you think might happen if you use/add/change...? why do you think it doesn't work? what elements/materials/tools/variables should you take into account? Or use analogies of processes that are closer or better known to the students.

Also in this process, as in the previous phase, group dynamics techniques can be used, such as Phillips 66 [1-3], or cooperative techniques, such as 1-2-4 [4-6], to share knowledge.

For better organization of the work of each group, the Kanban board [7-8] can be used, simply marking three columns: to do, in progress, and done. In each session, 3-5 minutes are dedicated at the beginning to locate the work done and to be done, decide what will be addressed in the session and distribute tasks, and another 3-5 minutes at the end to review what has been done. This will facilitate the development of each project.

To address the diversity of students in the classroom, tasks can be assigned within each group according to the abilities of each student (design/draw/build the model, search for information, get materials, etc.). Also, to encourage participation without gender bias, roles can be established in the groups (secretary, spokesperson, moderator, coordination, etc.) with well-defined functions that will rotate in each session.
